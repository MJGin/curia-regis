### Twinsectra Ltd v Yardley
- **Citation:** [2002] 2 AC 164 (House of Lords)
- **Court/Forum:** House of Lords
- **Year:** 2002
- **Principle:** Dishonesty for accessory liability is judged by the combined subjective and objective test: the defendant must have acted dishonestly by the standards of reasonable people and must have realised that.
- **Facts:** A claim against a solicitor who paid trust money to a client in breach of trust.
- **Holding:** Dishonesty for accessory liability is judged by the combined subjective and objective test.
- **Judges:** Lord Hoffmann (House of Lords)


### Foskett v McKeown
- **Citation:** [2001] 1 AC 102 (House of Lords)
- **Court/Forum:** House of Lords
- **Year:** 2001
- **Principle:** Beneficial ownership is a proprietary matter: a claimant who can trace property into its substitute may assert a proprietary claim in priority to the wrongdoer's creditors.
- **Facts:** A trustee misapplied trust funds to purchase a life insurance policy; beneficiaries claimed the proceeds.
- **Holding:** Beneficial ownership is a proprietary matter: a claimant who can trace property into its substitute may assert a proprietary claim in priority to the wrongdoer's creditors.
- **Judges:** Lord Browne-Wilkinson (House of Lords)


### Royal Brunei Airlines Sdn Bhd v Tan
- **Citation:** [1995] 2 AC 378 (Privy Council)
- **Court/Forum:** Privy Council
- **Year:** 1995
- **Principle:** Accessory liability for breach of trust requires dishonesty; negligence or knowledge of the breach is insufficient.
- **Facts:** A claim against a company director who assisted in a breach of trust.
- **Holding:** Accessory liability for breach of trust requires dishonesty; negligence or knowledge of the breach is insufficient.
- **Judges:** Lord Nicholls (Privy Council)

### Barnes v Addy
- **Citation:** (1874) LR 9 Ch App 244
- **Court/Forum:** Court of Appeal in Chancery
- **Year:** 1874
- **Principle:** A stranger to a trust is liable as a constructive trustee where he dishonestly assists in a breach of trust or knowingly receives trust property.
- **Facts:** A claim against solicitors who assisted a trustee in breaching the trust.
- **Holding:** A stranger to a trust is liable as a constructive trustee where he dishonestly assists in a breach of trust or knowingly receives trust property.
- **Judges:** Sir George Jessel MR (Court of Appeal in Chancery)
