### Oil Platforms (Islamic Republic of Iran v United States of America)
- **Citation:** [2003] ICJ Rep 161
- **Court/Forum:** International Court of Justice
- **Year:** 2003
- **Principle:** The use of force in self-defence must be necessary and proportionate; a State may not rely on self-defence where the necessity is not established.
- **Facts:** Iran claimed that the United States unlawfully attacked Iranian oil platforms.
- **Holding:** The use of force in self-defence must be necessary and proportionate; a State may not rely on self-defence where the necessity is not established.
- **Judges:** Shi, President (International Court of Justice)


### Articles on Responsibility of States for Internationally Wrongful Acts
- **Citation:** ILC Articles on Responsibility of States for Internationally Wrongful Acts (2001) UN Doc A/56/49(Vol I)/Corr 4
- **Court/Forum:** International Law Commission
- **Year:** 2001
- **Principle:** Every internationally wrongful act entails responsibility; conduct is attributable where an organ acts, or where instructions, direction or control are exercised; reparation must wipe out the consequences of the wrong.


### Gabčíkovo-Nagymaros Project (Hungary v Slovakia)
- **Citation:** [1997] ICJ Rep 7
- **Court/Forum:** International Court of Justice
- **Year:** 1997
- **Principle:** Necessity may preclude wrongfulness only in exceptional circumstances; a State may not rely on changed circumstances to evade a treaty obligation, and good faith governs performance.
- **Facts:** A dispute over a dam project on the Danube between Hungary and Slovakia.
- **Holding:** Necessity may preclude wrongfulness only in exceptional circumstances; a State may not rely on changed circumstances to evade a treaty obligation, and good faith governs performance.
- **Judges:** Schwebel, President (International Court of Justice)


### Military and Paramilitary Activities in and against Nicaragua (Nicaragua v United States of America)
- **Citation:** [1986] ICJ Rep 14
- **Court/Forum:** International Court of Justice
- **Year:** 1986
- **Principle:** A State is responsible where it exercises effective control over non-State actors; the prohibition on the use of force and the principle of non-intervention are rules of customary international law.
- **Facts:** Nicaragua claimed that the United States was responsible for military and paramilitary activities against it.
- **Holding:** A State is responsible where it exercises effective control over non-State actors; the prohibition on the use of force and the principle of non-intervention are rules of customary international law.
- **Judges:** Nagendra Singh, President (International Court of Justice)


### North Sea Continental Shelf (Federal Republic of Germany v Denmark; Federal Republic of Germany v Netherlands)
- **Citation:** [1969] ICJ Rep 3
- **Court/Forum:** International Court of Justice
- **Year:** 1969
- **Principle:** Customary international law requires both widespread and representative State practice and opinio juris; treaty provisions do not automatically crystallise into custom.
- **Facts:** A dispute over the delimitation of the continental shelf in the North Sea.
- **Holding:** Customary international law requires both widespread and representative State practice and opinio juris; treaty provisions do not automatically crystallise into custom.
- **Judges:** Tanaka, Vice-President (International Court of Justice)


### Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties
- **Citation:** Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (adopted 23 May 1969, entered into force 27 January 1980) 1155 UNTS 331
- **Court/Forum:** Multilateral treaty
- **Year:** 1969
- **Principle:** A treaty is interpreted in good faith in accordance with the ordinary meaning of its terms, in their context and in light of its object and purpose; travaux préparatoires are supplementary only.
