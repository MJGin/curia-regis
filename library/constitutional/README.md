### Mitu-Bell Welfare Society v Kenya Airports Authority & 2 Others

- **Court/Forum:** Supreme Court of Kenya
- **Year:** 2021
- **Principle:** Forced evictions must be carried out in a manner consistent with human dignity; the State must provide adequate notice, consultation and resettlement, and resource constraints cannot excuse indignity.

### Ndii & others v Attorney General & others
- **Citation:** [2021] KEHC 9746 (KLR) (13 May 2021) (Consolidated Petitions E282, 397, E400, E401, E402, E416 and E426 of 2020)
- **Court/Forum:** High Court of Kenya (Constitutional and Human Rights Division)
- **Year:** 2021
- **Source:** https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kehc/2021/9746/eng@2021-05-13
- **Principle:** A constitutional amendment process must comply with the Constitution's own architecture, including the requirement of public participation and the allocation of constituent power; a process that bypasses those requirements is invalid.
- **Facts:** Consolidated petitions challenged the Building Bridges Initiative (BBI) constitutional amendment process. The petitioners alleged that the process bypassed constitutional requirements. The case raised questions of public participation, constituent power and the amendment process.
- **Holding:** A constitutional amendment process must comply with the Constitution's own architecture, including public participation and the allocation of constituent power; a process that bypasses those requirements is invalid.
- **Judges:** Mwita, Ong'udi and Ngaah JJ (High Court)


### In the Matter of the Principle of Gender Representation in the National Assembly and the Senate
- **Citation:** [2012] KESC 5 (KLR) (11 December 2012) (Advisory Opinion Application 2 of 2012)
- **Court/Forum:** Supreme Court of Kenya
- **Year:** 2012
- **Source:** https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kesc/2012/5/eng@2012-12-11
- **Principle:** Article 81(b) creates a binding constitutional obligation to secure the two-thirds gender principle; its realisation is progressive and requires legislative and policy measures, not judicial legislation.
- **Facts:** The Attorney General sought an advisory opinion on the principle of gender representation in the National Assembly and the Senate. The reference concerned Article 81(b) of the Constitution and the obligation to achieve the two-thirds gender principle. The Court was asked to determine whether the principle was immediately enforceable or progressively realisable.
- **Holding:** Article 81(b) creates a binding constitutional obligation to secure the two-thirds gender principle; its realisation is progressive and requires legislative and policy measures, not judicial legislation.
- **Judges:** Mutunga CJ, Tunoi, Ibrahim, Ojwang, Wanjala and Ndung'u SCJJ (Supreme Court)

### Federation of Women Lawyers Kenya (FIDA-K) & 5 others v Attorney General & 3 others
- **Citation:** [2011] KEHC 2099 (KLR) (25 August 2011) (Petition 102 of 2011)
- **Court/Forum:** High Court of Kenya
- **Year:** 2011
- **Source:** https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kehc/2011/2099/eng@2011-08-25
- **Principle:** Customary law is subordinate to the Constitution; customary practices that deny women equal protection in succession and family matters cannot survive the guarantees of equality and non-discrimination.
- **Facts:** The Federation of Women Lawyers challenged customary law provisions on succession that discriminated against women. The petitioners alleged that customary practices denied women equal protection in inheritance matters. The case raised questions of the relationship between customary law and the Constitution.
- **Holding:** Customary law is subordinate to the Constitution; customary practices that deny women equal protection in succession and family matters cannot survive the guarantees of equality and non-discrimination.
- **Judges:** Mwilu J (High Court)
