### Francis Karioko Muruatetu & Another v Republic
- **Citation:** [2017] KESC 2 (KLR) (14 December 2017) (Petition 15 & 16 of 2015)
- **Court/Forum:** Supreme Court of Kenya
- **Year:** 2017
- **Source:** https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kesc/2017/2/eng@2017-12-14
- **Principle:** The mandatory death penalty is unconstitutional as it deprives the court of the discretion to consider the circumstances of the offender and the offence, violating the right to a fair trial and human dignity.
- **Facts:** Francis Karioko Muruatetu and another were convicted of murder and sentenced to the mandatory death penalty. They challenged the constitutionality of the mandatory death penalty. The case raised questions of sentencing discretion and human dignity.
- **Holding:** The Supreme Court held that the mandatory death penalty is unconstitutional as it deprives the court of discretion to consider the circumstances of the offender and the offence. The Court directed that sentencing guidelines be developed for murder cases.
- **Judges:** Maraga CJ, Mwilu DCJ, Ibrahim, Ojwang, Wanjala and Ndung'u SCJJ (Supreme Court)
