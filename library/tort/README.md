## Entries


### Poole Borough Council v GN
- **Citation:** [2019] UKSC 25, [2020] AC 780
- **Court/Forum:** Supreme Court of the United Kingdom
- **Year:** 2019
- **Principle:** A public authority does not owe a duty of care merely by exercising its statutory functions; liability requires an assumption of responsibility or a special relationship.
- **Facts:** A claim by children who suffered abuse in foster care against the local authority.
- **Holding:** A public authority does not owe a duty of care merely by exercising its statutory functions; liability requires an assumption of responsibility or a special relationship.
- **Judges:** Lord Reed (Supreme Court)


### Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police
- **Citation:** [2018] UKSC 4, [2018] AC 736
- **Court/Forum:** Supreme Court of the United Kingdom
- **Year:** 2018
- **Principle:** The police owe a duty of care in the ordinary way when their positive conduct creates a foreseeable risk of injury; the Caparo test applies to novel categories, not to established ones.
- **Facts:** A bystander was injured during a police chase of a suspect.
- **Holding:** The police owe a duty of care in the ordinary way when their positive conduct creates a foreseeable risk of injury.
- **Judges:** Lord Reed (Supreme Court)


### Michael v Chief Constable of South Wales Police
- **Citation:** [2015] UKSC 2, [2015] AC 1732
- **Court/Forum:** Supreme Court of the United Kingdom
- **Year:** 2015
- **Principle:** The police owe no duty of care to protect an individual from the criminal acts of a third party in the absence of an assumption of responsibility or a special relationship.
- **Facts:** A claim by the family of a woman murdered by her ex-partner after police failures.
- **Holding:** The police owe no duty of care to protect an individual from the criminal acts of a third party in the absence of an assumption of responsibility or a special relationship.
- **Judges:** Lord Toulson (Supreme Court)


### Caparo Industries plc v Dickman
- **Citation:** [1990] 2 AC 605 (House of Lords)
- **Court/Forum:** House of Lords
- **Year:** 1990
- **Principle:** A duty of care arises only where damage is reasonably foreseeable, the relationship is sufficiently proximate, and it is fair, just and reasonable to impose the duty.
- **Facts:** Shareholders relied on an auditor's report to buy shares; the company collapsed and they sued the auditors.
- **Holding:** A duty of care arises only where damage is reasonably foreseeable, the relationship is sufficiently proximate, and it is fair, just and reasonable to impose the duty.
- **Judges:** Lord Bridge (House of Lords)


### Hill v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire
- **Citation:** [1989] AC 53 (House of Lords)
- **Court/Forum:** House of Lords
- **Year:** 1989
- **Principle:** The police owe no general duty of care to individual members of the public in the investigation and suppression of crime; policy considerations preclude such a duty.
- **Facts:** A claim by the mother of a victim of the Yorkshire Ripper against the police.
- **Holding:** The police owe no general duty of care to individual members of the public in the investigation and suppression of crime; policy considerations preclude such a duty.
- **Judges:** Lord Keith (House of Lords)


### Donoghue v Stevenson
- **Citation:** [1932] AC 562 (House of Lords)
- **Court/Forum:** House of Lords
- **Year:** 1932
- **Principle:** A manufacturer owes a duty of care to the ultimate consumer to take reasonable care that goods are free from defects likely to cause injury, the neighbour principle being the foundation of the tort.
- **Facts:** A consumer became ill after drinking ginger beer containing a decomposed snail; she sued the manufacturer.
- **Holding:** A manufacturer owes a duty of care to the ultimate consumer to take reasonable care that goods are free from defects likely to cause injury.
- **Judges:** Lord Atkin (House of Lords)
